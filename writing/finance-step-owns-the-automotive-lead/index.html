Automotive digital journey · Saudi Arabia

With most Saudi car purchases financed, the finance step owns the lead

The digital journey is built backwards. It starts with configuring a car and ends at affordability — which is the question the customer actually had first.

Published 2026-08-21 · Alawi AlAlawi, Digitalization Manager, BMW Group KSA, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia

The numbers behind this

  • Roughly 70% of car purchases in Saudi Arabia are financed, and Islamic structures account for around 65% of that financing.
  • Third-party platforms began running AI-assisted valuation and finance pre-qualification ahead of the dealer during early 2026.

Look at how an automotive digital journey is sequenced almost everywhere in this region. Choose a model. Configure it. Pick a color. Book a test drive. Submit an enquiry. Somewhere near the end, financing.

Now look at how the customer actually sequences it. Roughly seventy percent of purchases in Saudi Arabia are financed. Islamic structures are around sixty-five percent of that. For most buyers the first real question is not which trim — it is what monthly payment they qualify for, and therefore which cars are even in the conversation.

The journey is built backwards. It answers the last question first and the first question last.

Why this is getting more expensive, not less

For years this cost you sequence and little else. The customer worked out affordability at some point in the process, usually with your finance desk, and the lead stayed yours throughout.

That changed during early 2026, when third-party platforms started running AI-assisted valuation and finance pre-qualification ahead of the dealer. The customer can now resolve affordability — and often trade-in value — before contacting anyone.

When that happens, one of two things follows. Either the lead arrives later, better informed and considerably less winnable, because price and payment were settled elsewhere by someone whose incentive was not your margin. Or it does not arrive at all, because the platform routed it to whoever pays for placement.

Either way the importer has been disintermediated at the exact step that determines the shortlist.

What this argues for

Integrating finance pre-qualification into the importer's own journey is worth more than another chat channel. Not a finance application — a pre-qualification: an indicative payment range, early, on your own property, in exchange for the enquiry.

The mechanics matter. It has to be indicative rather than binding, so it does not trigger a credit event or set an expectation the bank later refuses. It has to be structured for the products people actually use, which in this market means Islamic structures as the default path rather than a checkbox. And it has to write back into the CRM, so the sales conversation opens with the payment range already known rather than rediscovering it.

Done that way it changes what a lead is. Instead of a name and a model preference, you get a name, a model preference and a budget band — which is the difference between a lead a salesperson has to qualify and one they can serve.

The strongest argument against this

Financing is a regulated product with its own approval chain, and the bank controls the outcome. An importer that fronts pre-qualification takes on friction and compliance exposure for a step it does not ultimately own. It may also introduce a new drop-off point: a customer who was going to enquire now sees an indicative number they dislike and leaves, when previously a salesperson would have found a structure that worked.

That is the real risk. Pre-qualification can shrink the funnel at the top in exchange for quality further down, and if the indicative model is poorly calibrated it shrinks it for no gain at all.

What would change my mind

Data showing conversion is flat or worse where finance pre-qualification is integrated into the OEM or importer journey, versus referred out to a partner. That is a measurable comparison and somebody has run it.

I would also change position if regulatory guidance made indicative pre-qualification impractical to offer without a full application — at that point the friction argument wins on its own.

Absent either, the sequencing problem stands. The step that decides which cars are in the conversation is currently the last step in the journey, and increasingly it is happening on somebody else's property.
