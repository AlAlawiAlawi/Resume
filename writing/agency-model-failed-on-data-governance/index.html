The agency model did not fail on retail format. It failed on data governance

Volkswagen paused, JLR reverted, Ford and Lotus went back. Mercedes-Benz UK grew. Same format, opposite outcomes — which means the format was never the variable.

Published 2026-08-21 · Alawi AlAlawi, Digitalization Manager, BMW Group KSA, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia

The numbers behind this

  • Volkswagen put its agency rollout on hold following a strategic review; JLR reverted to a refined franchise model; BMW adjusted its timeline; Ford and Lotus returned to franchise.
  • A majority of surveyed UK dealers now believe the agency transition is over.
  • Mercedes-Benz UK grew under the same model: registrations up 20% and private retail up 29%, with consultation with retail partners, premium positioning and leaving used cars with the retailer cited as reasons.

The European agency-model retreat is usually read as a verdict on direct sales. Volkswagen put its rollout on hold after a strategic review. JLR reverted to a refined franchise. BMW adjusted the timeline. Ford and Lotus went back. Survey a room of UK dealers today and most will tell you the move is over.

That reading is wrong, and the reason it is wrong sits in the same dataset.

Mercedes-Benz UK grew under agency. Registrations up twenty percent, private retail up twenty-nine percent. Same format, opposite outcome, same market, roughly the same period.

When one variable produces both outcomes, it is not the variable.

So what was the actual variable?

The reasons cited for the Mercedes result are not format reasons. They are readiness reasons: consultation with retail partners before imposing the model, a premium position that could sustain fixed pricing, and a decision to leave used cars with the retailer rather than absorbing them.

Strip those down and two of the three are governance. Fixed pricing only works if you can hold a price across every channel and every touchpoint simultaneously — which is a data-consistency problem before it is a commercial one. Leaving used cars with the retailer is a decision about which party owns which records and which margin, made deliberately rather than by default.

The agency model moves the customer of record from the retailer to the manufacturer. That is the whole change. Everything else — margin structure, stock ownership, pricing authority — follows from it. An OEM that makes that move without being able to hold a single, consistent, current customer record across its own network has taken on the accountability without the capability.

Why this matters more in the Gulf than in Europe

Most Gulf markets run through importers rather than through OEM national sales companies. The importer sits between the manufacturer's standards and the retail network's operations, and it is where the customer record actually lives.

That makes the importer the party that determines whether an OEM retail standard is achievable in-market. When a manufacturer publishes a standard — response times, data fields, lead handling, a consistent price — the importer either has the record structure to comply or it does not. The standard does not create the capability.

So the European agency story is not distant news here. It is a preview of what happens when an OEM assumes readiness that was never measured. The format arrives; the data does not.

What an importer should take from this

Before adopting any OEM retail standard that assumes a single customer view, the question worth answering is whether one exists. Concretely: can you produce one customer number that survives both a board review and an OEM audit, and still survives four quarters later?

That last clause is the hard part. Producing a clean number once is a data-cleansing exercise. Producing one that stays clean is a governance regime — de-duplication that runs continuously, edit controls, mandatory fields, audit logging, role-based access. Programs that skip it get a good quarter and then drift.

The strongest argument against this

One success against many retreats is survivorship. Mercedes may have succeeded because of brand strength and product cycle timing, and the governance explanation may be a story told afterward to explain what was really a market-position advantage. Premium brands can hold fixed prices that volume brands cannot, and that alone might account for the whole gap.

What would change my mind

A second and third clean success under agency at brands without Mercedes' pricing power. That would confirm the variable is readiness rather than position.

Or the reverse: evidence that the Mercedes gain tracked its product cycle rather than its retail model. If the growth maps onto launches, the governance argument is decoration.

Until then, the useful lesson from Europe is not that agency failed. It is that retail format changes expose data capability, and the exposure is unforgiving.
